Dental emergency? What to do right now

Simple first steps until you can see a dentist — and the signs that mean you should go to a hospital straight away.

In pain or had an accident? Call us and we'll try to see you as soon as possible.

Call the clinic now · +91 72754 33783

Go to a hospital emergency department now if you have

  • Swelling spreading to the eye, neck or under the jaw
  • Difficulty breathing or swallowing
  • High fever with tooth or face swelling
  • Bleeding that doesn't stop after 30 minutes of firm pressure
  • A hard blow to the face or jaw, or a possible broken jaw

Severe toothache

  1. Rinse with warm salt water (half a teaspoon of salt in a glass of water).
  2. Gently floss to remove any food stuck between teeth.
  3. Hold a cold pack on the outside of your cheek for 15 minutes at a time.
  4. Take a painkiller you normally use safely, as directed on the pack.
  5. Don't put aspirin, clove oil soaked cotton or any tablet directly on the gum — it can burn.
  6. Book a visit soon: pain that wakes you at night usually needs treatment.

Knocked-out adult tooth

  1. Act fast — the best chance is within 30–60 minutes.
  2. Hold the tooth by the crown (white part), not the root.
  3. If dirty, rinse briefly with milk or water. Don't scrub it.
  4. If you can, push it gently back into its socket and bite on a clean cloth.
  5. Otherwise keep it in a small cup of milk (or inside the cheek for adults) and call us now.
  6. A knocked-out milk (baby) tooth should not be put back — just see us.

Broken or chipped tooth

  1. Rinse your mouth with warm water.
  2. Save any broken pieces in milk or water.
  3. Use a cold pack if there's swelling.
  4. Avoid chewing on that side and very hot or cold food.
  5. See us within a day or two — sooner if there's pain or the break is large.

Swelling of the gum or face

  1. This often means an infection — call us the same day.
  2. Use a cold pack outside the face; don't use heat.
  3. Don't try to pop or squeeze a gum boil.
  4. If swelling spreads to the eye or neck, or you have fever or trouble swallowing, go to a hospital.

Bleeding after a tooth extraction

  1. Bite firmly on a clean gauze or cloth pad over the socket for 30–45 minutes.
  2. Keep your head raised; don't spit, rinse hard or use a straw today.
  3. A slight ooze for a day is normal.
  4. If heavy bleeding continues after 2 rounds of pressure, call us or go to a hospital.

Lost filling or crown, broken braces wire

  1. Keep a lost crown safe and bring it with you.
  2. Cover a sharp edge or poking wire with orthodontic wax or sugar-free chewing gum.
  3. Don't glue a crown back with household glue.
  4. Book a visit in the next few days.
