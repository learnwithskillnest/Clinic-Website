Food for healthy teeth, skin and hair

Everyday Indian food habits that help — from a doctor trained in nutrition.

Dr. Pallavi is a dental surgeon and aesthetic physician with a PG Diploma in Nutrition, so food is part of every plan for teeth, skin and hair. These are simple, everyday Indian food habits that help — no expensive supplements needed.

For healthy teeth and gums

  • How often matters more than how much sugar. Sweets, biscuits, sweet chai and juice sipped through the day keep teeth under attack. Have sweets with a meal, not as snacks.
  • Sticky sweets like toffee, chikki and gulab jamun syrup cling to teeth — rinse with water after.
  • Calcium: milk, curd, paneer, ragi and til (sesame) support teeth and bones.
  • Crunchy vegetables and fruit — carrot, cucumber, apple, guava — clean teeth naturally and need chewing.
  • Vitamin C for gums: amla, guava, oranges, lemon. Bleeding gums can be a sign of poor gum health — see us rather than only changing diet.
  • Water after meals and tea washes away sugar and acid. Fluoride toothpaste twice a day still matters most.
  • Tobacco, gutkha and paan stain teeth, cause gum disease and mouth cancer. Ask us for help to quit.

For clear, healthy skin

  • Protein at every meal — dal, chana, rajma, curd, paneer, eggs, fish or chicken — helps skin repair.
  • Colourful vegetables and fruit (carrot, spinach, tomato, papaya, amla) give antioxidants and vitamins A and C.
  • Healthy fats: a handful of nuts, flaxseed (alsi), walnuts or fish support the skin barrier, especially for dry skin.
  • Acne: for some people, lots of sugary foods, refined flour (maida) and large amounts of milk can make breakouts worse. Try cutting down for 6–8 weeks and see.
  • Water: 2–3 litres a day for most adults, more in summer. Drinking more water won't cure dry skin on its own — a moisturiser still helps.
  • Sun protection matters more than any food for pigmentation and ageing.

For hair fall

  • Hair is made of protein — low protein intake is a common cause of thin hair.
  • Iron: green leafy vegetables, beans, dates, jaggery in small amounts, and meat if you eat it. Have them with a vitamin C food (lemon, amla) to absorb more.
  • Vitamin D: 15–20 minutes of morning sun; many people in India are low.
  • Crash diets often cause heavy hair fall 2–3 months later.
  • Don't start iron, biotin or vitamin D tablets without checking — a simple blood test shows what you actually need.

For children

  • No milk or juice bottle in bed — it's a common cause of decay in toddlers.
  • Offer water between meals instead of juice or soft drinks.
  • Keep sweets for after meals; choose fruit, roasted chana or makhana as snacks.
